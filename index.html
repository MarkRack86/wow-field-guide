        How the talent rules work
        • You get your first talent point at level 10 and one more each level, so 51 at level 60. Each rank of the Legacy perk Talented moves the first point a level earlier, down to level 5.
        • Each row needs 5 more points spent higher up in the same tree: 5 for row 2, 10 for row 3, and 30 for the bottom row.
        • Talents joined by a line need the talent above or beside them filled first.
        • Tap a talent to read it, then tap it again or use + to add a point and − to take one away. With a keyboard, + and − work on the focused talent. With a mouse, right-click removes a point.
        • A gold border means some points and a gold fill means maxed. A faded talent is still locked.
        • Dual Specialization arrives at level 40, when you have 31 points. Each healing plan's first 31 points make a complete healing spec, and the rest go in as you level.
        • Every plan was checked point by point, so each step is legal at its level. Match plan to my level sets the build to the plan's first points for your level, and Undo puts your own build back.
        • Once your character is synced from Blizzard, the questing spec shows the talents you really have, and the tree can’t be changed while that’s on. Turn it off to plan freely: your own build is kept separately, and the sync updates at about 4am and 6pm UK time, from your last logout.
        • Builds are saved in this browser only.

          Dungeons

          Show

            Times and how each dungeon feels come from Classic runs at the right level, except where beta players reported on Forever's new dungeons. Forever reworked boss loot and quest rewards in the original dungeons, so named drops are Classic favourites, not promises.

            How dungeons work in Forever
            • Monsters inside give little XP. Most of the reward is in each dungeon's one-time quests, so pick them up before you go and run each dungeon once.
            • There's no automatic Group Finder. List a group or browse the Group Browser, message the leader, then travel to the entrance yourself.
            • Meeting stones can't summon. Only a Warlock's Ritual of Summoning can, with the Warlock and two others at the entrance.
            • Nine dungeons are new to Forever. Level ranges are the in-game Group Browser ones, from Warcraft Tavern's dungeon list.
            • There are no Heroic or Mythic versions: each dungeon has one difficulty.
            • Every dungeon drop unlocks its look for transmog, and dungeons drop Tarnished Undermine Reals, a currency.
            • Clearing a dungeon's Spelunker challenges earns Legacy Points.
            • Once you've finished a dungeon's quests, repeat runs give much less XP than questing outside.

            Arrives in 3 days

            Mon 9 Nov to Sun 15 Nov

            A week each month, opening on the Monday after the first Friday. It alternates between Elwynn Forest and Mulgore, and barkers in Ironforge and Orgrimmar announce it. Sayge's fortune gives a buff, and turning in trade goods earns prize tickets.

            November's dates come from the game's data.

            Raids open

            In 33 days

            Wed 9 Dec

            Barrow Deeps (10 players), Hyjal Summit (20) and Onyxia's Lair (40), all for level 60 characters. Blizzard hasn't given a time.

            Rare monsters and named quest targets respawn on their own timers, and there's no world boss schedule yet. Respawn speed varies by zone, with starting zones the fastest, and herbs and ore go to whoever reaches them first. None of it runs on a clock you can track. The timer worth watching at your pace is your own rested XP: it builds while you're logged out in an inn or a city, up to a level and a half of bonus XP.

            Gold

            Making gold with Herbalism and Alchemy, level by level. The section for your level opens by itself, and the details follow your class and faction. Prices come from the latest Auction House snapshot, so the crafts and herb prices here change every day.

            On the beta, gathering while questing made about 1 to 2 gold an hour around level 20, so a slow start is normal. It climbs with level: in high-level zones, grey items alone sell for a few gold each.

              By level

              Shaman tip

              Your class tip appears here.

              At every level

              • Look up prices before you sell or craft. The Market tab reads Blizzard’s Auction House data twice a day and says whether each material is worth more sold, crafted or vendored, and AHledger shows prices for the other factions and rulesets.
              • Sell to the players who are buying. Low-level herbs sell in the first weeks after launch, while top-end materials listed early mostly sit.
              • Craft only when it pays: a potion has to sell for more than its herbs and vial would. What to craft, at the top of this tab, does the sums with the latest prices.
              • Post short auctions and match the cheapest price rather than undercutting it. On Forever the newest listing at a price sells first, and the market covers everyone on your ruleset, so a fair price sells within hours. Longer auctions cost a bigger deposit.
              • Never vendor Scarce herbs, Herb Seeds, or greens and blues you haven't priced.
              • Don't equip a Bind on Equip item you mean to sell: once worn, it can't be traded.
              • Cook what you kill. Every Well Fed food adds 5% to XP from kills, so cooked food sells from day one.
              • Train only the spells you use, and keep your savings for riding at 40.

              Forever's market is new, so prices aren't settled yet: check the Auction House before you sell or craft. The details come from beta and launch guides by Warcraft Tavern, Conquest Capped, wow-professions.com, Blizzard Watch and Icy Veins, listed at the bottom of the page.

                      Crafted items

                      What each craft sells for, after the 5% cut, against what its materials cost. Herbs count at what they’d sell for, as on the Gold tab; vendor items count at the cheaper of the vendor and the Auction House. Prices use the cheapest 20 listed.

                        How the Auction House works

                        One market for your whole ruleset
                        • Forever has no realms. Your market is every Horde player on the Normal ruleset in Europe, so there are far more sellers than on a Classic realm and price gaps close within hours.
                        • There are Horde, Alliance and neutral Blackwater auction houses. In Classic the neutral house took 15% of a sale against 5% at a faction house, so sell through your own faction's.
                        • Materials are pooled: buyers take any quantity from any seller, so splitting stacks doesn't help.
                        Posting: durations, deposits and the cut
                        • Auctions run for 2, 8 or 24 hours. The deposit grows with the length and you lose it if nothing sells, so short auctions are usually the better deal.
                        • The deposit is a share of the item’s vendor value, bigger for longer auctions, and you get it back when the auction sells. For now every listing needs at least 1s, even for herbs worth a few copper, so you lose that much on anything that expires. Blizzard says the minimum is going back to 1c; until then, post cheap materials in one big listing or sell them to a vendor.
                        • When something sells, the house keeps 5% and mails you the rest along with your deposit.
                        • If a vendor pays more than the Auction House, sell to the vendor.
                        Pricing: match, don't undercut
                        • On Forever the newest listing at a price sells first, so matching the cheapest price sells as fast as undercutting it and stops prices sliding.
                        • Prices far below the market are bought and relisted within minutes, so dumping only feeds resellers.
                        • Check the Crafted items list before you craft to sell: a craft only pays when it sells for more than its materials would.
                        The economics of a new market
                        • Demand follows the players. Low-level materials sell in the first weeks, while top-end materials listed early mostly sit.
                        • Common herbs fall in price quickly as gatherers pile in. Rarer ones, such as Frilled Lichen, hold their value.
                        • Low-level crafts often sell for less than their materials, because everyone levelling a profession is selling the same things.
                        • From 9 December, raid nights add demand for flasks, potions and food.
                        • Mister Graphed in Riverglades sells rare materials, Black Lotus and Arcane Crystal included, at fixed prices with no limit, which caps what they can fetch.
                        Where these prices come from
                        • Blizzard's own Auction House data for the Horde house on the Normal ruleset. Blizzard refreshes it about once an hour, and the scheduled refresh reads it at about 4am and 6pm UK time, keeping one price a day.
                        • How fast things sell is an estimate from two one-hour samples a day, around 4am and 6pm UK time. Each compares two of Blizzard’s hourly snapshots and counts the listings that shrank or vanished while they still had hours to run. Buyers take the cheapest first, so a listing that vanished while a cheaper one stayed untouched counts as cancelled, not sold. A cancelled listing at the cheap end still counts, so treat it as a rough upper guide.
                        • Each price is the average of the cheapest 20 listed: close to what a small stack costs, and to the price you'd match to sell. "Lowest" is the cheapest single listing.
                        • The middle of everything listed isn't used, because big stacks posted far above the market drag it up without selling. One morning a big stack of Earthroot at 1s 99c put the middle at four times what Earthroot really cost.
                        • Vendor prices come from Forever's item data. Vials are sold in fives, so an Empty Vial works out at 4c.

                            Herb zones

                            Where your Herbalism earns the most. Each zone is scored on how many herbs you can pick there for its size, and what they sell for today, so a zone packed with good herbs beats a big empty one.

                            Best zones for you

                            The score compares herb value per square kilometre, with 100 for the best zone you can farm. "Per herb" is what a typical herb you can pick there sells for after the Auction House's 5% cut. Herbs in brackets need more skill.

                            ZonePer herbScore

                              How the score works
                              • Every herb has a set of spawn points in each zone, taken from Questie's Forever data. A zone's herb mix shows how often each herb turns up there.
                              • Each spawn point you can pick counts at its herb's price, using the average of the cheapest 20 listed today, less the 5% cut. Herbs with fewer than 20 listed aren't priced, so they don't add to the score.
                              • The total is divided by the zone's area from the game's own map data. A dense zone means less riding between herbs.
                              • Only some spawn points have a herb at any moment, and the area counts water and mountains too, so treat the score as a guide. Other gatherers matter as much as the map: quieter zones and quieter hours pay better.
                              • Zones for the other faction are left out. Questie's data has no herb spawns for Forever's new zones yet, so they aren't listed.
                              • Bonus finds aren't in the score, because their drop rates aren't known. They're worth far more than common herbs, so zones rich in Earthroot, Mageroyal, Briarthorn and Stranglekelp are better than their score suggests.

                              Bonus finds

                              Forever adds scarce materials and Herb Seeds that sometimes come with a normal herb. They don't grow on their own, and they sell for far more than the herbs they come from. Prices are after the 5% cut.

                                5. Wed 4 NovLaunch build

                                  Launch notes: what affects your play

                                  Questing and interface

                                  • Learning a spell no longer puts it on your action bars, and new bags no longer equip themselves.
                                  • Quest objectives have tooltips and map markers.
                                  • Quest XP was adjusted. Monsters respawn faster in low-level zones, fastest in starting zones, and Blizzard plans to slow low-level zones to more typical rates once the launch rush settles.
                                  • Ruins of Lordaeron quests need level 15. "Holy Diver" now gives XP, and "Carnage" is fixed.
                                  • Some satyrs now count as demons.
                                  • Class training for every rank from 1 to 60 costs about 206 gold in total.
                                  • Gamepad support works, but Blizzard still calls it a beta and is updating its interface.

                                  Combat

                                  • Every class deals more damage, and higher-level monsters have more health.
                                  • Low spell ranks no longer lose their chance to trigger talents. The exception is the Mage spell Blizzard: ranks learned more than 12 levels below you still trigger Arcane Concentration, Frostbite and Fingers of Frost less often.
                                  • Racials: Orc Command is back, Blood Fury has a 2-minute cooldown, Berserking lasts 15 seconds, Touch of the Grave can trigger every 15 seconds, and racial weapon specializations are gone.
